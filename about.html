A Hundred Years

The Southern History department was established in 1926 with a gift from the Tutwiler family. The Archives followed in 1976. On April 11, 2027, the Research Library turns 100.

For a century, the building at 2100 Park Place has stood as the intellectual and cultural treasury of Birmingham and Alabama. It anchors the research mission of the Birmingham Public Library, part of the Jefferson County Library Cooperative’s network of 40 libraries.

Today, restored murals in the reading room are becoming part of the building’s new chapter.

1926

Southern History department established

Nearly 95,000 items covering Southern art, architecture, and genealogy, including more than 50,000 volumes and 3,700 printed family histories. Recognized by Family Tree Magazine as one of the best libraries in the country for genealogists.

1927

The iconic building at 2100 Park Place opens

1938

Property Files begin

Every standing structure in Birmingham has been documented ever since — used today for historic markers, probate questions, and plain curiosity.

1976

The Archive established

Government, business, civil rights, and environmental history from the Birmingham region. More than 50 million documents and photographs.

2027

The Research Library turns 100

On April 11, 2027, a century after the Southern History department was established, the Library celebrates its Centennial.

Notable Holdings

There is always more to find in the collection. A few of the treasures:

Miss Iwate, the Japanese Friendship Doll.

One of 58 Friendship Dolls exchanged between the United States and Japan in the 1920s as a symbol of goodwill. Miss Iwate has one of the most complete sets of belongings of any surviving doll.

A rare signed copy of To Kill a Mockingbird.

Harper Lee’s classic, signed, in the story’s home state.

A 1607 Geneva Bible.

A complete 1607 translation, inscribed with the names and birthdates of the family members who owned it in the late 17th and early 18th centuries.

The Birmingham City Jail Dockets.

Spanning 1893 through the 1970s, they capture pivotal moments in Birmingham civil rights history.

The Edith London Scrapbooks.

A detailed chronicle of the life of an upper-middle-class girl and woman from the 1880s through the 1930s.

The Property Files.

Every standing structure in Birmingham has been documented since 1938. These files are used daily for historic markers, probate questions, and plain curiosity.

The Community Memory Lab

Bring your box in. Take it home.

The Community Memory Lab will be free to use. You will not need an appointment, and nothing you bring will stay with the Library unless you want it to. If you choose to, you can donate a digital copy of your materials to the Archives, adding your family’s story to Birmingham’s. The equipment, scanning, and digital hosting are all free.

What can you bring?

Photographs, letters, documents, slides, and home audio recordings. Bring what matters to your family. If you are not sure whether something can be digitized, bring it anyway and ask.

Want to know when it opens?

The Memory Lab is not open yet. It will be on the third floor of the Research Library downtown, once the renovation is complete. Leave your name and email, and we will let you know when it is ready.
